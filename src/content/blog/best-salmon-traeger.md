I've made a lot of salmon. This was the best of it, so I'm writing down exactly
what I did before I forget the details and can't repeat it.

The whole meal was salmon on the Traeger, a rice pilaf, grilled asparagus, and
blistered shishito peppers. All of it below.

## The salmon

**Prep:**

- Lay the fillet skin side down on a sheet of tin foil.
- Season the top with Malibu seasoning.
- Add a generous amount of lemon juice. More than feels right.
- Lay sliced lemons across the top.

**Cook:**

- Heat the Traeger to **350F**. I used Kirkland Signature pellets, the Oak,
  Hickory, Maple, and Cherry blend.
- Put the salmon on (still on the foil) and cook **34 minutes, untouched.** No
  flipping, no checking, no poking.
- Pull it at **135F in the thickest part.** That was the perfect doneness for
  this cook, moist and just flaky.

My fillet was about 1 to 1.5 inches thick in the center and tapered thinner
toward the sides. The 34 minutes got it to 135F for this fillet, but thickness
is what really drives it, so go by temperature over time. A cheap probe
thermometer takes the guessing out of it.

The untouched part matters. Leaving it alone is what gave it that even, flaky
finish.

## Rice pilaf

Based on [Sarah's Rice Pilaf](https://www.allrecipes.com/recipe/203951/sarahs-rice-pilaf/)
from Allrecipes. I'm writing the whole thing out here rather than just linking,
in case that page ever disappears. **My one change: I used beef stock instead of
chicken broth.** It's a deeper, richer flavor and it's what made this side stand
out.

Serves 4. About 10 minutes of hands-on time, 50 minutes total.

**Ingredients:**

- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1/2 cup orzo pasta
- 1/2 cup diced onion
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1/2 cup uncooked white rice
- 2 cups beef stock (the original calls for chicken broth)
- 1/2 teaspoon salt

**Steps:**

1. Melt the butter in a skillet with a lid, over medium-low heat. Add the orzo
   and cook, stirring, until it turns golden brown.
2. Stir in the onion and cook until it turns translucent.
3. Add the garlic and cook for 1 minute. Stir in the rice and beef stock, turn
   the heat to high, and bring it to a boil. Add the salt.
4. Reduce heat to medium-low, cover, and simmer until the rice is tender and the
   liquid is absorbed, 20 to 25 minutes.
5. Take it off the heat and let it stand, covered, for 5 minutes. Fluff with a
   fork before serving.

## Grilled asparagus

Done on a regular gas grill, not the Traeger.

- Cut about 1 inch off the ends of 1 bunch of asparagus.
- Put the asparagus in a gallon bag with **1/4 cup lemon juice, 1/2 cup shredded
  parmesan, and 1/8 cup olive oil.** Toss to coat.
- Grill over **low heat on the top rack for 15 minutes.**

## Blistered shishito peppers

These were the surprise of the night. So good, and about the easiest thing on
the table.

- Heat a pan over **medium.**
- Spritz in a little **avocado oil** and season with **garlic salt.**
- Add the shishitos and cook until they blister, tossing now and then.
- Spritz **fresh lime juice** over the top right at the end.
- Eat them right away, while they're hot. That's when they're best.

## Notes for next time

- The heavy lemon on the salmon, both the juice and the sliced lemons on top, is
  a big part of why it was so good. Don't go light on it.
- Beef stock in the pilaf over chicken broth is a keeper.
- Everything finished around the same time, which was lucky more than planned.
  The salmon runs 34 minutes and the pilaf 25 to 30, so start the salmon first,
  then the pilaf, then the asparagus last.
